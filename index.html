What is a Magic Square?

A magic square is an n×n grid of distinct positive integers where every row, column, and both main diagonals sum to the same value — the magic constant.

🔬 Key Discovery

Magic squares are characterized by zero covariance between position and value. They are the unique ground states of a natural energy functional — isolated minima in a vast landscape of arrangements.

The Zero-Covariance Theorem

Theorem: An arrangement S is a magic square if and only if:

\[ E(S) = \text{Cov}(X,Z)^2 + \text{Cov}(Y,Z)^2 + \text{Cov}(D_{\text{main}},Z)^2 + \text{Cov}(D_{\text{anti}},Z)^2 = 0 \]

This energy functional measures the "imbalance" of an arrangement. Magic squares achieve perfect balance.

Theorem B: Extended Moment Vanishing

All third-order cross-moments vanish for magic squares:

\[ \mathbb{E}[X^2 Z] = \mathbb{E}[Y^2 Z] = \mathbb{E}[XYZ] = 0 \]

This extends the zero-covariance property to higher-order moments, suggesting a deeper statistical balance.

Theorem D: Modular Preservation

Magic squares remain "magic mod p" under reduction modulo any prime p:

\[ S \text{ magic with } M(n) \implies S \mod p \text{ magic with } M(n) \mod p \]

Verified for n = 3, 5, 7 and p = 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13.

Conjecture: Refined Gap Scaling

The minimum perturbation gap scales as:

\[ \Delta_{\min} \cdot n^4 \approx c \quad \text{(constant)} \]

This suggests O(1/n⁴) scaling, not O(1/n²) as previously thought.
